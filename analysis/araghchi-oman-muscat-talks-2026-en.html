Araghchi’s Visit to Oman on November 17: Can Muscat Reopen US-Iran Talks?

Araghchi’s visit to Oman on November 17 puts Muscat’s diplomatic channel in focus. Assess the prospects for US-Iran talks and what could confirm a next step.

Araghchi’s visit to Oman was announced while the distinction between regional consultations and formal negotiations between Iran and the United States remained important. On November 16, Muscat Daily reported, citing Iran’s official news agency, that Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi was expected to meet his Omani counterpart, Badr Albusaidi, the following day. The announcement describes a planned meeting between the two foreign ministers. For Oman’s diplomatic role, the immediate question is whether their conversation will lead to a specific proposal passing between Tehran and Washington and whether the two sides share an understanding of the next step.

Araghchi’s visit to Oman and the history of the Muscat channel

Oman’s Foreign Ministry had already reported a November 5 phone call between the ministers. Muscat also hosted talks involving Araghchi and US presidential envoy Steve Witkoff in April 2025. That experience shows that Oman can provide a framework for contact when both sides agree. However, the issues under dispute, the authority of representatives and the domestic cost of compromise can change substantially from one round to the next. Established access is an advantage at the start of a process that still depends on political choices in both capitals.

At the first stage, the value of the trip may lie in clarifying messages. A public statement intended for a domestic audience can differ from a proposal that officials are authorized to negotiate. For example, an intermediary can ask which conditions must be considered before a meeting and which could be addressed during talks. The answers would help both sides assess whether there is enough common ground to agree on an agenda or whether further exchanges are needed first.

Moreover, Oman can ask about the authority of the people expected to carry messages. Acceptance by an envoy becomes more meaningful when the other side knows who can approve implementation. Consequently, agreeing on the level of representation and responsibility for answering a proposal is part of the work that precedes negotiations. A written understanding of how messages will be transmitted can also reduce disputes over whether a statement represents a formal position.

The nuclear issue and the order of commitments

An assessment of Araghchi’s visit to Oman must consider the political conditions surrounding the nuclear file. Both parties might be willing to discuss monitoring or limits on nuclear activities while disagreeing about when economic pressure should ease or when measures that raise tensions should stop. The sequencing can therefore matter as much as the content. Each side needs to know what it would receive after taking the first step and which institution could confirm that the step occurred.

One possible approach would be a limited agreement with a clear start and end date. For instance, the parties could discuss actions that can be checked promptly and reserve more complex disputes for a later stage. However, even a short timetable would be difficult if neither side would accept an interim commitment before the final outcome was settled. Any limited arrangement would depend on the participants’ ability to record changes and address disputed interpretations in time.

Araghchi’s visit to Oman also offers an opportunity to clarify how maritime security relates to nuclear talks. Decisions concerning commercial navigation can have immediate consequences for governments and companies. Nevertheless, assessing shipping conditions and verifying nuclear commitments require different information and institutions. Oman could help the parties consider whether separate discussions would make a limited understanding easier or whether either side regards progress on the two issues as inseparable.

Another question concerns an unexpected incident while consultations are under way. If it takes too long for an official account to reach the other side, both governments may make decisions using incomplete information. Therefore, an agreed channel for clarifying what happened and carrying official messages would have practical value. It could give officials time to examine evidence and decide whether the incident warrants a change in their negotiating position.

What to watch after Araghchi’s visit to Oman

First, a statement that specifies the scope of the consultations and the next action would provide evidence about the process. General references to reducing tensions indicate Oman’s approach. In contrast, confirmation that a specific proposal was exchanged provides a firmer basis for tracking what follows. If only one party discloses details, comparing its account with statements from the other participants becomes particularly important.

Second, watch whether Araghchi’s visit to Oman leads to agreement on a date, the level of representation or a venue for another meeting. A date gives the process a deadline. The level of representation indicates who may have authority to negotiate, while agreement on a venue can show that the participants have accepted a channel for contact. However, each of these indications depends on whether the delegates attend and are authorized to address the issues under discussion.

Third, cooperation among mediators matters. Qatar and other regional governments may be able to reach different actors. When they relay consistent messages, several channels can help maintain communication during periods of limited direct contact. Conversely, incompatible versions of a proposal may lead officials to believe that the other side has made a commitment it has yet to approve. Recording the wording and timing of a message would help prevent that misunderstanding.

Ultimately, Araghchi’s visit to Oman offers a chance to assess whether both sides are ready to move beyond regional consultations. The clearest evidence would be a step that Tehran and Washington can each confirm. That could be agreement on a first topic, a timetable or a process for contacting one another after an incident. Moreover, evidence that both sides are willing to use the agreed channel would make Muscat’s contribution easier to judge. Until there is confirmation from the principal parties, the development to track is Oman’s consultation with Iran and what the participants say they intend to do next.
