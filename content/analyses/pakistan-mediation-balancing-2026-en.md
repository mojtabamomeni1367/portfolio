Islamabad is trying to reduce tensions between Tehran and Washington while managing its defense relationship with Riyadh. On October 30, Reuters reported that Pakistan's military spokesperson was cautiously optimistic about its mediation efforts. Several days earlier, army chief Asim Munir had traveled to Tehran with officials from Saudi Arabia and Türkiye. However, Reuters reported on November 3 that Iran and the United States remained far apart. The gap shows the limits of what Islamabad can achieve by bringing officials into contact.

Pakistan's mediation matters when its access to different governments allows it to turn a broad message into a workable proposal. Its proximity to Iran, defense relationship with Saudi Arabia and contacts with Washington help Islamabad hear competing concerns. At the same time, those ties shape how others interpret its motives. The policy faces three tests: the reliability of its messages, the clarity of its defense commitments and its ability to coordinate with other intermediaries.

## Pakistan's Mediation and the Reliability of Messages

An intermediary is most effective when governments know which messages it is carrying on someone else's behalf and which proposals reflect its own reading of the talks. There is a practical difference between saying another government will consider a proposal and saying it has accepted one. The first leaves room for discussion, while the second creates an expectation that a commitment will be fulfilled. Consequently, a promising proposal can turn into a dispute over what was promised if that distinction is lost in transmission.

In this case, Islamabad's diplomatic role could help clarify the sequence of steps. Reuters reported on November 3 that contacts continued and differences over Tehran's conditions remained. In these circumstances, practical progress could mean identifying what each government would do, when it would do it and who would confirm that it had done so. For example, if a maritime measure depends on an economic restriction being lifted, an intermediary needs to distinguish a public announcement from an executive instruction and an observable result.

Success also depends on the ability to convey unwelcome news. An intermediary with close contacts on both sides may be tempted to understate their differences so talks continue. That can secure another meeting. However, it may do more damage to trust if officials later discover that they understood the same text differently. Brief written records, confirmation that messages have been received and an explicit account of issues that remain open can reduce that risk.

## Pakistan's Mediation and the Defense Commitment to Saudi Arabia

Saudi Arabia and Pakistan signed a strategic mutual defense agreement in September 2025. Their published statement treats aggression against either country as aggression against both. Moreover, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Türkiye signed the [Mecca defense agreement](https://momenimojtaba.com/analysis/tehran-meeting-mecca-pact-2026-en.html) in August 2026. These commitments are evidence of cooperation. The response to any particular incident, however, still needs to be assessed from public decisions and specific evidence. The signature on an agreement alone does not establish that Islamabad has decided to take part in a particular operation.

From Tehran's perspective, the credibility of Pakistan's mediation depends in part on how clearly its diplomatic role can be distinguished from its defense decisions. Diplomatic backing, training and direct military participation have different effects. If their boundaries are unclear, an adversary may interpret a limited measure as a sign of broader involvement. Conversely, explaining the scope of a decision can reassure Riyadh that cooperation continues while allowing Tehran to assess Pakistan's conduct against observable actions.

The balance has domestic costs for Islamabad as well. A decision understood abroad as loyalty to a partner could prompt questions at home about resources, border security and the scope of involvement. On the other hand, excessive caution may lead Riyadh to question what defense cooperation offers in a crisis. Addressing both concerns depends on the scope of commitments and how the governments consult each other. Broad assurances can conceal differences for a time, but they cannot substitute for clear decisions once a specific incident occurs.

## Pakistan's Mediation Alongside Other Diplomatic Channels

Qatar also plays a role in contacts between Iran and the United States. For Islamabad, cooperation with Doha can help preserve continuity when one channel becomes harder to use. Multiple intermediaries are useful when each can convey complementary information or reach officials whom others have difficulty accessing. However, if competing drafts circulate without coordination, governments may develop expectations through one channel that were never accepted through another. Identifying an authoritative text and assigning responsibility for following up on each issue can keep the parties from mistaking different versions for firm commitments.

Accordingly, Pakistan's mediation should be assessed by its effects on observable decisions. Narrowing differences over one implementation step, keeping communication open after an incident and clarifying who confirms an action are more concrete indicators than the number of visits or calls. If Islamabad pursues these outcomes in coordination with other intermediaries, its relationship with Riyadh and access to Tehran can help it understand both governments' concerns. It can then convey those concerns in terms that allow negotiators to test whether a specific compromise is possible.

The main test comes when one side asks for quick action and another warns of consequences. In that situation, Pakistan's mediation depends on Islamabad's ability to convey objections accurately, explain the limits of its defense role and preserve a channel for talks. A mechanism that specifies response times and responsibility for each step allows governments to judge their decisions against what the other side actually does. Without these details, the distance between a promise of de-escalation and the response to a new incident will remain wide. Moreover, clear records can make it possible to resume talks after a setback without reopening every issue that had already been settled.
